Friday gig prep

Assigned to Lou Headstock, due Friday. Holly Humbucker checks the setlist, and the drummer promises (again) to bring the cymbals by Wednesday.

The band booked this gig without telling you, so here is the totally rad catch-up plan: five rehearsals over two weeks, and no more surprises.

Day 1: restring with fresh flatwounds, tune to drop D and play the root notes of every song until your fingers say thank you.

Day 2: learn the new song the guitarist wrote, which has eleven chords, four key changes and one very long solo that is not yours.

Day 4: practice the slap part in the bridge, with a short video that shows how to look cool while nobody watches the bassist.

Day 7: full rehearsal with the band, where somebody will ask you to turn it down and somebody else will ask why you can't be heard.

Day 14: soundcheck at the venue, where the sound engineer calls you the tall guitarist and the drummer gets all the credit.

Keep the pedalboard under 10 pedals, with one tuner and no more than two fuzzes. Pack the spare strings last, once the cables are coiled.

Send the setlist to the merch table before Thursday. They sell the shirts, so they need to know which songs have a bass solo (zero).

Test every cable and the low B on the 5-string, in the rehearsal room and on the actual stage, before the doors open at eight.
